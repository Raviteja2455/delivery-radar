How to read this

Each stock's bar shows the day in one line.

The bar's length is today's volume against its 20-day average; the dashed line marks a normal day (1×) and the bar is full at 2.5×. The dark part is the share that was delivered. The black tick marks the 5-day average delivery %. A long, mostly dark bar on an up day is the footprint of real buying.

The score counts six signals:

    The live strip shows GIFT Nifty, Brent crude in US$ (WTI underneath) and USD/INR, with MCX gold (₹ per 10 g) and silver (₹ per kg) below, refreshed every 20 seconds while the app is open. A green dot means that market is trading now. GIFT Nifty, USD/INR and MCX are real time; crude runs 10 minutes behind. Each uses its most-traded contract, named after the label. When MCX trading moves to the next month ahead of expiry, the label changes month and the price shifts by the gap between the two contracts. Outside NSE hours the line under the strip compares GIFT Nifty with the close of the Nifty futures for the same month, which is the usual read on how the next session will open.

    The band at the top sets the size of swing trades. All of them are in cash, with no MTF. Green (an uptrend) means half size, because breakouts taken in green markets did worst in the 2020–26 backtests; amber and red mean full size. The Swing tab runs the full entry, sizing and exit rules on every stock and explains the evidence under The rules this tab applies.

    The Picks tab ranks every Nifty 100 stock each night and names one three-month swing pick every ten days, aiming for at least +5%. It then tracks every pick, and a replay of the same rule on past data, so you can see how often the target was reached. The Swing tab stays on the Nifty 50, the stocks its plan was tested on. Log the picks you buy there, like trades on the Swing tab: every nightly update refreshes each one's gain and says when the rule would sell.

    The Research tab screens the Nifty 500 (large, mid and small caps) for investments of six months to two years. It keeps only profitable, well-financed, growing companies, works out a fair value four ways, and names a stock only when the price is well below it and no longer falling. It names every stock that qualifies, each sized at ₹1 lakh, and follows every pick to its exit; which ones you take depends on your budget. Most nights it names nothing: waiting for price is part of the method.

    The Deals tab lists bulk and block deals by FIIs (and, if you choose, by Indian mutual funds and insurers) in every NSE stock: who bought or sold, how much, at what price, who was on the other side, and whether the stock is holding above the price they paid. Filter by side, period, index, size and deal type.

    FII activity, inside the band, shows each session's net cash buying or selling by foreign investors (NSE's provisional figure) and how their index futures, calls and puts changed, from NSE's participant-wise open interest. A positive futures or calls number, or a negative puts number, is the bullish side. The verdict under the table weighs the latest day's cash, futures and options: when they all agree it says bullish or bearish, otherwise it leans with the majority. On Nifty's weekly expiry (marked *), option changes include contracts that simply expired. The tile beside VIX shows how much of FIIs' index-futures position is long.

    Source: NSE end-of-day files (security-wise delivery, F&O bhavcopy, bulk and block deals, index closes, participant-wise F&O open interest and FII/DII figures). Updated every weekday night.

    Live strip: TradingView quotes (crude delayed 10 minutes), with CNBC and Moneycontrol as backups.
